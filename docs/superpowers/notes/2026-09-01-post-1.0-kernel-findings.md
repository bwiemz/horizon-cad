# Post-1.0 kernel findings (Phases 81–83)

Notes from the first block of work after the 80-phase roadmap closed. With no
phase list left to follow, the work is driven by the gaps the README's Feature
Maturity table names — starting with the one that made every other rating hard
to trust: nothing in the kernel checked that a solid's *geometry* made sense.

## Phase 81 — the validation gap was wider than the caveat suggested

The README already carried the caveat that the B-Rep validators are
"combinatorial only". Writing `GeometryValidator` and pointing it at the
existing kernel turned that from a caveat into two concrete bugs.

**Sharp cones were degenerate.** `PrimitiveFactory::makeCone` builds box
topology from two 4-point rings. With `topRadius = 0` the top ring's four
points coincide, so the solid carries four zero-length edges around a
zero-area cap. Euler and manifold both pass — nothing in the structural
checks can see it. `MainWindow`'s Cone command asks for exactly that shape
(`makeCone(5, 0, 10)`), so every cone a user inserted was geometric nonsense.
Fixed by giving the sharp case apex topology (base quad + four triangles,
5V/8E/5F), sewn with `SolidSewer`, carrying the same analytic conical surface
the frustum path binds. `makeCone` now also refuses a cone with both radii or
zero height instead of returning a degenerate solid.

**ChamferOp's defect got a precise name.** The pinned defect note said the
volume integrator "disagreed"; the validator says what it disagreed about —
four non-planar loops, two self-intersecting loops and one zero-area face on
the 20mm-box case.

### What the validator does *not* do

Planarity is only asserted for faces whose carrier is planar (no surface, or
a surface whose normal field is constant under sampling). Curved carriers —
cylinders, spheres, tori, fillet blends — are skipped rather than guessed at,
because deciding whether a loop vertex lies *on* a trimmed NURBS patch needs
a closest-point solve whose result is meaningless outside the patch's uv
domain. Two checks are advisory and never fail the gate: edge curves that do
not run between their edge's vertices, and distinct vertices at the same
position (legal at a cone apex or a sewn seam, but usually a missed weld).

## Phase 82 — the chamfer defect was two bugs, not one

The known defect was recorded as a reconstruction problem. It was that, but
the polygon soup handed to the reconstruction was *also* wrong, and the second
bug is the one that produced the wrong volume.

1. **Loop construction.** The rewrite walked each original loop half-edge by
   half-edge and pushed `cur->origin` for every non-chamfered edge — including
   at vertices the chamfer had already replaced. A face adjacent to a
   chamfered edge therefore kept the very corner the chamfer cut away,
   alongside the offsets that replaced it. Loop construction is now
   vertex-driven.
2. **Reconstruction.** The soup was replayed through Euler operators by an
   iterative convergence loop that picked target faces by vertex-set
   containment. It terminated with valid linkage — just not the linkage the
   polygons described. Replaced with `SolidSewer`.

**Found in passing: the kernel disagrees with itself about winding.**
`PrimitiveFactory`'s loops are wound *inward* by the Newell convention, while
`SolidSewer` documents its input as outward-wound. Both work, because every
consumer (mass properties, tessellation) normalizes the sign globally — but it
means any code that orients a new face against a fixed "outward" reference is
correct for only half the kernel. ChamferOp's new face is oriented against the
loop area vectors of the two faces it bridges, which is right under either
convention. This is worth a deliberate decision later; for now it is written
down.

FilletOp needed no rebuild: its Phase-61 rewrite already assembles half-edges
strictly from a polygon soup, and it passes the geometric validator across the
constant-radius, variable-radius and corner-blend paths. It picked up the same
output gate.

## Phase 83 — vertex blends are a clipping problem, not a patching one

The natural way to read "chamfer two edges that share a vertex" is that some
extra corner patch has to be generated where the chamfer faces meet. For
planar chamfers that is wrong: the material a chamfer removes is the
half-space beyond its chamfer plane, so the corner is simply the original
corner clipped by the planes of every chamfer meeting there. Three equal
chamfers at a box corner give three planes meeting at a single point, and each
chamfer face becomes a pentagon — no corner face at all.

That reading collapses the whole operation into one code path: clip a local
wedge at each touched vertex, clip each chamfer face by the chamfers at its
ends, sew. The single-chamfer case is the same code with one clipping plane,
so it did not need a separate branch.

The clip runs on a *wedge* — the corner's two loop directions extended far
enough that every cut lands well inside it — rather than on the whole face.
A chamfer plane is unbounded; clipping a whole face by it would let a chamfer
at one corner slice a distant part of a non-convex face.

Verified against inclusion–exclusion volumes for the union of the cutting
prisms (`(1/2)d²L` each, `d³/3` per pair, `d³/4` per triple):

| case | volume |
|---|---|
| one edge of a 10³ box, d = 2 | 980 |
| two edges at a corner | 962⅔ |
| three edges at a corner | 946 |
| all twelve edges (the chamfered cube, 18F/32V) | 808 |

**Remaining limits**, documented in the header rather than papered over: the
offset directions come from the adjacent faces' normals, so an oblique corner's
offsets do not land on the third face's plane — such input is refused by the
geometric gate rather than silently mis-built. The wedge clip assumes a convex
corner. Inner face loops are not carried through the rewrite.

## Phase 84 — the curved-geometry gap was not where the docs said it was

The plan coming out of Phase 83 was analytic surface–surface intersection, on
the strength of the README's note that "curved faces participate as their
inscribed loop polyhedra". Measuring first changed the diagnosis entirely.

`makeCylinder(5, 10)` returned **V=8, E=12, F=6** — box topology — with a
cylindrical NURBS patch bound to its four lateral faces. The solid was a
square prism to every computation in the kernel; only the renderer thought
otherwise. Measured against the analytic values:

| primitive | reported | analytic | error |
|---|---|---|---|
| cylinder r5 h10 | 500.0 | 785.4 | −36% |
| sphere r5 | 192.5 | 523.6 | −63% |
| frustum 2/1 h5 | 23.3 | 36.7 | −36% |
| torus R10 r3 | **0.0** | 1776.5 | −100% |
| cylinder − cylinder (bore) | 320.0 | 502.7 | −36% |

SSI would not have helped any of this: it intersects surfaces, and these
operands' edges are straight lines between four corner points. The surfaces
were decoration.

### Why loop refinement is not the fix either

The obvious cheap fix — follow each edge's curve when extracting the boundary
— was tried and rejected on measurement. The cylinder's rim edges *do* carry
exact quarter arcs (Phase 61), and refining them takes the caps to proper
64-gons. The volume still plateaued at 594 against 785.

The reason is `BoundaryMesh::signedVolume`'s fan from `points[0]`. A fan is
exact for a planar loop and for the enclosed volume of *whatever surface it
describes* — but a refined lateral band is not planar, and the fan from its
first vertex describes a cone from that vertex, not the cylindrical band. The
mesh was watertight (all directed edges paired, area vectors summing to zero)
and still enclosed the wrong volume, because "the volume enclosed by a
non-planar loop" is not defined until you fix a triangulation. Faithful
evaluation of a curved face needs the face tessellated over its carrier, not
its boundary refined.

### What shipped

Faceting at construction, which is the coherent answer for a kernel whose
Boolean, classification and mass-property paths are all faceted anyway. The
B-Rep becomes what the kernel already treats it as, at a resolution the caller
controls, and every consumer converges at once — no second evaluation path, no
watertightness puzzle across faces with different carriers, and no new
inconsistency between what mass properties reports and what Booleans compute.

Volumes now converge from below (an inscribed facet hull is always smaller),
which the tests assert as a *property* — error strictly positive and strictly
decreasing with resolution — rather than pinning a magic number.

### Found in passing: the renderer was drawing each sphere six times

`SolidTessellator` emits a face's whole **untrimmed** carrier whenever that
carrier is curved. With one surface shared across six faces, a sphere was
drawn six times over: 480,000 triangles for a shape that needs 960, and a mesh
enclosing 3× the solid's volume. The cylinder drew its lateral surface four
times, 320,004 triangles against 124. Faceted primitives take the loop path,
so each face is emitted exactly once; both the per-face count and the
mesh/solid volume agreement are now pinned by tests.

### The carrier-versus-intent distinction

Faceting alone would have cost two real features. `MateGeometry` fits a
cylinder to a face's bound surface — a planar facet gives a planar frame, so
concentric mates would need all 32 facets picked. `DrawingDimensioner`'s
radial measurement looks for an edge whose curve is circular — a chord is not.

Both are fixed by separating two things the kernel had conflated:
`topo::Face::surface` is the **carrier**, the surface the loop actually lies
on, and `topo::Face::analyticSurface` (with `topo::Edge::analyticCurve`) is
the **ideal geometry the facet approximates**. Binding the cylinder to a
planar quad as its carrier would have put display and computation back out of
step — exactly the bug being fixed. Recording it separately keeps design
intent available to feature recognition, so one pick on one facet still
resolves the cylinder it belongs to.

These fields are not serialized, so the distinction is lost across a save and
reload; recovering it would mean either persisting them or refitting on load.

### Two consequences, pinned rather than papered over

- **A torus fails `checkEulerFormula()`.** It is genus 1, so `V - E + F` is 0,
  and the check carries no genus term. `checkManifold()` and the geometric
  validator both pass. There is no way to validate Euler for a genus > 0 shell
  without knowing the genus independently, so the tests assert the false
  result deliberately rather than weakening the check.
- **STEP export of a cylinder no longer emits a rational cylindrical
  surface.** The facets are planar, so the file is exactly the model in
  memory. Writing the recorded analytic surface would put a cylindrical
  carrier under a planar quad loop — geometry the file would contradict.
  Emitting analytic faces means un-faceting on export: a real STEP-fidelity
  feature, and a separate one.

## Phases 85–86 — the blends could not act on what Phase 84 built

Faceting the primitives immediately raised the question of whether the blend
operations still worked on them. They did not, and the reasons were defects
rather than missing features.

### Capacity checks were measuring a proxy

Both ops capped the offset at half the shortest edge of the adjacent face.
That is a stand-in for "do not overrun the neighbouring geometry"; it holds
for a box and collapses on faceted geometry, where the shortest edge is the
facet chord. On a 32-sided cylinder of radius 5 the chord is 0.98, so every
chamfer over 0.49 was refused — while the geometry is in fact exact past 4.9.
It was wrong for boxes too, rejecting a 6mm chamfer on a 10mm cube that builds
correctly and integrates exactly.

What actually bounds the offset is how far the face reaches along the offset
direction. That is a *necessary* condition — it never rejects a distance that
would have worked — and the geometric gate added in Phase 82 is what does the
guaranteeing. The usable range on a cylinder rim went from 0.49 to the cap's
inradius (4.976), bounded by geometry instead of a heuristic.

### Three tolerances, three different meanings

The stubborn part was a set of refusals reading "self-intersecting loops" at
moderate chamfer distances. Tracing them found no crossing at all: a loop
segment of **1.075e-7**.

Three components disagreed about what one vertex is:

| component | tolerance |
|---|---|
| the clip's deduplication | 1e-9 × scale |
| `SolidSewer`'s weld | 1e-7, absolute |
| `GeometryValidator`'s degeneracy test | relative to the loop's extent (≈1e-6 here) |

A 1.075e-7 segment is therefore too long to weld, too short to be a real edge,
and reads as degenerate — so it sewed as legal and validated as broken. The
clip now deduplicates against the loop's extent, which is the scale the
validator uses and the scale the inputs are actually built to: an offset
shared by two edges is only equal to within the rounding of two independent
constructions.

Two supporting fixes came out of the same trace. Intersections are snapped to
the endpoint they land on, because a cut through an existing vertex recomputes
that vertex as `cur + (nxt - cur) * t` with t at one end of its range, where
cancellation leaves it a fraction of a micron off. And a segment nearly
parallel to the clip plane no longer has an intersection computed at all: the
denominator there is the difference of two nearly equal signed distances, so
the result is noise rather than a point.

**Worth remembering:** an absolute tolerance and a scale-relative one will
disagree somewhere, and the gap is exactly where the confusing bugs live.

### The fillet had Phase 84's disease

Checking FilletOp for the same capacity problem turned up something larger. A
fillet emitted **one flat quad** joining the two tangent lines, and bound it a
correct rational-quadratic arc surface — degree 2 in V with three control
points, a genuine cylindrical patch. Its *loop* was the chord.

Since every loop-based path in the kernel evaluates a solid from its face
loops, mass properties, Booleans, classification and export all integrated a
chamfer while the renderer drew a fillet. Filleting one edge of a 10mm cube
removes `(1/2)r²L` instead of `r²(1 − π/4)L` — **2.33× too much material, at
every radius**. This is the same shape of defect as the faceted primitives:
an analytic surface pasted onto a coarse loop, so display and computation
disagree. It had gone unnoticed because the fillet tests checked face counts,
TopologyIDs and bound surfaces, but never a volume.

The fix mirrors Phase 84. Blends are faceted across the arc, sampled by
spherical interpolation between the two tangent radii so the points are exact
on the arc rather than a subdivision of the chord, and the arc patch is kept
on `Face::analyticSurface`. Two knock-on pieces were needed: the *end* faces
have to receive the whole arc chain rather than its chord, or the shell does
not close; and corner blends must follow the same samples, since the spherical
patch spans three arcs that are no longer straight.

Volume converges quadratically (0.286% → 0.078% → 0.020% → 0.005% per
doubling at r = 1), which the tests assert as a property — error positive and
strictly decreasing — rather than pinning a number.

Fixing this also surfaced that `analyticSurface` was dropped when a face was
carried through a later operation, so a blend from an earlier fillet lost the
arc it approximates. Faces now propagate it.

## Not yet addressed

Analytic surface–surface intersection remains future work, but it is a
fidelity improvement rather than a correctness fix: Booleans on curved solids
produce the right answer to within the operands' facet error.

Open items in this area:

- Filleting a *faceted* edge chain (a cylinder rim) is still refused.
  ChamferOp handles it; FilletOp's vertex blends require exactly three edges
  meeting at a corner, and a rim vertex has two. The chamfer work suggests the
  shape of the fix — treat the chain as one logical edge — but the fillet's
  rolling-ball construction is not a clipping problem.
- `analyticSurface` / `analyticCurve` are still not serialized, so the
  distinction between carrier and intent is lost across a save and reload.
- Faceting is applied at construction only. A solid loaded from disk keeps
  whatever resolution it was saved at, and there is no re-faceting pass, so
  primitive resolution is not a parametric property of a feature.
