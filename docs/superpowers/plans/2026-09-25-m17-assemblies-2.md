# Milestone 17 — Assemblies, part 2 (Phases 158–161)

Roadmap: [2026-09-25-professional-workflows-roadmap.md](../specs/2026-09-25-professional-workflows-roadmap.md),
findings A1–A4.

## Baseline (checked in the code)

- **A component is a part.**
  - `ComponentInstance` has a `partPath`, a `transform`, `suppressed`, one
    `cachedMesh` and a `resolvedPart` (a `Document`).
  - Insert Component takes `*.hzpart` only.
  - An assembly file placed as a component shows nothing, and no message
    says so. `loadPartMesh` gives nothing for it, and `PartLoader` refuses
    it.
- **"Held" means a `Fixed` mate** (`isFixed` in `AssemblyWorkbench.cpp`).
  No component carries the solver's `grounded` flag. With no `Fixed`
  mate, the solver grounds the first component, and says so.
- **Mates reference faces only** (`MateReference{componentId, faceId}`),
  planar or cylindrical.
  - `MateGeometry::frameForFace` samples a 3 × 3 grid. It keeps a point
    when that point's normal is degenerate, and drops the normal. So the
    least-squares points and normals fall out of step at an apex or a
    pole, and cones and spheres are refused.
  - There are no limit mates, and the solver has no inequalities.
  - An unknown mate type in a file reads as `Coincident`.
- **The mate → frame → solve bridge lives in the UI**
  (`AssemblyWorkbench::solveAssemblyMates`), with a hand-made copy in
  `tests/integration/test_AssemblyMates.cpp`.
  - Every edit resolves every component and scans faces by string.
  - The solver runs with its dense rank diagnostics on (O(n³)), and
    `rebuildScene()` clears and rebuilds the scene and the tree.
- **Components move only by typed values** (Move and Rotate Component).
  - A left drag in an assembly tab is a 2D box selection.
  - There is no 3D manipulator. The ViewCube's hit areas come from a cache
    filled while painting, so the offscreen tests, which never paint,
    cannot click it.
- **The BOM has one level** (`BomGenerator`, CSV `Item,Part,Quantity,Path`).
- **There are no exploded views, component patterns or mirrored
  components.** No camera or view state is saved in any file.
- **One format version (22) for every document type.** Each change to what
  a file holds bumps it, with a line in NativeFormat's history.

## Phase 158: Drag with the mates

### 158a: one mate solve for everyone, and a drag (as built)
- **`doc::AssemblyMates`** (new, in document):
  - `gather(assembly)` takes each component where it is, and each mate's
    frames, found once in the resolved parts. For a mate on geometry that
    cannot be found, it says "mate N references geometry that could not be
    resolved".
  - `solve({hold, diagnostics})` holds a component where a drag puts it.
  - What grounds the assembly without a hold (a Fixed mate, else the first
    component) still grounds it with one. Without that, a hold that broke a
    mate would move the base to meet it.
  - `place(transforms)` makes the next solve start where the last one left
    everything.
  - `AssemblyWorkbench::solveAssemblyMates` and the integration test both
    use it; the copies of the frame-finding code are gone.
- **The viewport** (`ViewportWidget`, `ComponentDragger`):
  - With the select tool, a plain left press on a component arms a drag.
  - Past Qt's start-drag distance the dragger takes it; a press and release
    in place still chooses the component.
  - The release of a press the drag took never reaches the tool.
  - Escape during a drag hands it back.
  - `pickModelPoint()` gives the world point a press grabs.
- **`AssemblyWorkbench`** is the `ComponentDragger`.
  - The component moves in the plane through the point grabbed, facing the
    view.
  - At each move it is held where the cursor puts it, with no diagnostics.
    If the mates cannot hold it there, it is let go from there and slides
    as far as they allow. If not even that, the last placement stays.
  - The nodes move in place, with no scene rebuild.
  - Release is one undo step ("Drag Component"), and none is recorded if
    nothing moved. Escape restores the snapshot taken at the press.
  - A component held by a Fixed mate is refused, with the reason in the
    status bar.
- **Tests:**
  - `ToolDriver` gets `pressAt`, `dragAt` and `releaseAt`.
  - UI: follows the cursor and keeps the mates, and is one undo step;
    slides as far as the mates let it; held by a Fixed mate; Escape; a
    click chooses.
  - Document: `AssemblyMatesTest`.
  - A preset view keeps the camera's distance, which put the eye inside a
    part in the first attempt, so the tests place the camera with
    `lookAt`.

### 158b: the triad (as built)
- **`ui::Triad`** has an arrow along each of a component's axes, and a ring
  about each, at the middle of its bounds.
  - It is `kPixels` long on screen at any zoom. The size is measured across
    the view at the triad's middle.
  - `hitTest` gives the handle nearest the press, within `kTolerance`
    pixels; on a tie the arrow wins. `handlePoint` gives a point on a
    handle.
  - `segments` gives what is drawn.
  - All of it is worked out from the camera when asked for, never cached
    while painting, so the offscreen tests, which never paint, press it.
- **`ComponentDragger::triadPose()`**: the first component chosen in the
  view (clicked, or chosen in the tree), its middle and its axes. The
  viewport draws its triad over the model, with no depth test.
- **A press on a handle** is looked for before a press on a component. It
  arms the drag with the handle, and a release in place chooses nothing.
- **The dragger constrains the motion:**
  - by an arrow, to the axis line: the ray's nearest point on it, from
    where it was grabbed;
  - by a ring, to a turn about the axis through the triad's middle: the
    signed angle, in the ring's plane, from the point grabbed.
  - The mates are then solved as for a free drag, held first, then let go.
- **Test:** the x arrow moves the lid along x only, even when the cursor
  strays sideways. The z ring turns it a quarter turn about its middle,
  still on the base. Nothing chosen, no triad.

## Phase 159: Subassemblies (as built)

- **An assembly can be a component.**
  - A component whose file is an assembly (`ComponentInstance::isAssembly`,
    by its extension) is read from its file for its components alone
    (`resolvedAssembly`). The parent shows what is saved.
  - Its components are resolved the same way (Lightweight or Resolved),
    recursively. A part it places that cannot be read is left out.
  - Its mesh is `AssemblyDocument::drawingMesh()`: its components' meshes
    placed and merged, each face and edge named `c<id>/...` as
    `drawingSolid()` names them. Resolved, its solid is `drawingSolid()`
    (`assemblySolid`).
  - `ComponentInstance::solid()` gives a part's solid or a subassembly's,
    and every user of a component's solid takes it: mates, interference,
    STEP export, and Add Mate's list of faces.
  - A subassembly is rigid in its parent, placed as it was saved.
- **Cycles.** `DocumentManager::resolveComponent` takes `within`, the
  canonical paths of the assemblies a component is inside. An assembly that
  places one of them, or itself, at any depth, is refused with the chain
  ("outer is placed inside itself (outer > inner > outer)").
  - Insert Component passes the assembly's own path, so an unsaved insert of
    itself is caught too.
  - Unsaved elsewhere, a loop is found in the files, one level deeper.
- **Picks and mates** go through the single node a subassembly already has:
  its merged mesh's names are its components'. A mate on
  `c<id>/<face>` finds the face in its gathered solid. No scene tree, and
  no tag prefix on `SceneNode`, was needed.
- **The BOM has levels** (`BomKind`):
  - top level: a subassembly is one line;
  - indented: its own lines under it, numbered 2.1, 2.2..., each quantity
    what one of it holds;
  - parts only: every part at every depth, each placement of a subassembly
    counted.
  - `BomLine` has `level`, `index` and `assembly`.
  - The dialog chooses among the three. An indented CSV gains a `Level`
    column, and its items are the dotted numbers.
- **STEP export** writes a subassembly's parts with the rest, in one level,
  their transforms composed with its own. A subassembly written as one part
  would have been a single solid of many shells, which reads back as one
  body.
- **Drawings** of an assembly draw a subassembly's gathered solid, and
  follow every file at every depth.
- **Living updates.**
  - `refreshComponentsOf(path)` matches a component that places the file at
    any depth, so a part changed deep inside refreshes each assembly it is
    in.
  - Saving an assembly refreshes the assemblies that place it.
- **The tree** shows a subassembly's components under it, recursively.
  Those rows are shown, not acted on; each is edited in its own assembly's
  tab.
- **Insert Component** offers parts and assemblies.
- **Files:** format version 23, because an older build would show the
  parent without its subassemblies.
- Not done:
  - a subassembly's own mates are not solved in the parent, which takes it
    as saved;
  - a subassembly open in a tab with unsaved edits shows its saved state in
    a parent;
  - the parts list on a drawing of an assembly stays top level.

## Phase 160: More to mate

- **References.** `MateReference` gets a kind: face, edge, vertex or datum.
  A datum is named by its part's `datum_<n>` feature id. Files keep
  `faceTag` for faces and add `kind` and `tag`.
- **Frames.** `MateFrameKind` gets these kinds:
  - `Line`: a straight edge, a datum axis, or the axis of a cylinder or
    cone;
  - `Point`: a vertex or a datum point;
  - `Circle`: a round edge, as its centre, axis and radius;
  - `Conical`: apex, axis and half-angle;
  - `Spherical`: centre and radius.
  - Before any of this, the sampling misalignment in `frameForFace` is
    fixed.
- **The solver dispatches residuals on the mate type and the two frame
  kinds.**
  - Coincident: point–point, point–line, point–plane, line–line and
    line–plane.
  - Concentric: axes, and sphere centres.
  - Distance: between points, from a point to a plane or a line, and
    between parallel lines.
  - Angle: between lines or planes, written with atan2 so it holds at 0°
    and 180°.
  - `expectedRankFor` follows the same pairs.
- **Limits.** A Distance or Angle mate may carry a `min` and a `max`.
  - The solver solves without the limits first. Each limit that is broken
    is then held at the bound it broke, and the mates are solved again,
    up to a few rounds (an active set).
  - A limit that cannot be met fails the solve, and names the mate.
- **Add and Edit Mate** take clicked edges, and a list of the components'
  datums, and have limit fields. The tree names the new kinds.
- **Files:** a version bump. Unknown mate types stop reading as
  Coincident: they are skipped, and the report says so.

## Phase 161: Exploded views and patterns

- **Exploded views** are named, and each is a list of steps: components,
  a direction and a distance.
  - They are kept in `AssemblyDocument` and in `AssemblyState`, so undo
    covers them.
  - Showing one moves the nodes by a display transform on top of each
    component's own. It never writes `comp.transform`, and mates are
    neither solved nor saved exploded.
  - Assembly ▸ Exploded Views lists them. The user adds a step from the
    selection, and chooses a view to show, or none.
- **Component patterns**, linear or circular, of one or more seed
  components.
  - The instances are placed from the seeds, so they follow a seed that
    moves. They get ids of their own, for picks, interference and the
    BOM, where they are counted.
  - A pattern is one feature of the assembly. It is edited as a whole,
    with instances left out by index, as a part pattern does.
- **Mirrored components need a mirrored body** (a transform with a
  negative determinant breaks STEP export, `Pattern::transformed` and
  interference). They come after Milestone 18's mirror of bodies (Phase
  162), as a mirrored part placed by a rigid transform. That is a move
  from this phase, and it is noted in the roadmap.
- **Files:** a version bump.
