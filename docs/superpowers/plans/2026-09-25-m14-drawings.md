# Milestone 14 — Drawings from the model (Phases 147–150)

Roadmap: [2026-09-25-professional-workflows-roadmap.md](../specs/2026-09-25-professional-workflows-roadmap.md).

## Baseline (checked in the code)

The drawing library exists, but nothing in the application reaches it.
- `DrawingProjection::project` draws **every B-Rep edge** (`DrawingProjection.cpp:159`).
  - A curved face is facets, so a cylinder draws each seam between two of
    them: 32 lines down its side.
  - A curved face has **no silhouette**. A cylinder seen side-on is drawn by
    its facet seams, not by its two outlines.
  - Each chord of a rim is a straight line, so a circle is a 32-gon, though
    every chord records the arc it replaces (`analyticCurve`).
- **Visibility is brute force**:
  - every edge is cut into 24 pieces, however short it is, so a
    2,048-facet cylinder's edges make about 150,000 rays;
  - each ray is tested against every triangle;
  - a curved edge's run becomes one straight segment from its first point
    to its last.
- **The Front view looks from behind.** Its direction is −Y, so the eye is
  at +Y and X runs leftwards. The viewport's Front, and every drawing
  standard, look along +Y. Top, Right and Isometric agree with the viewport.
- `populateDrawing` (a drawing into a 2D document, by layer) is local to
  `DrawingExport.cpp`. A `.hzdwg` keeps only the part's path and a gap.

## Phase 147: Clean projection

### As built
- **Each edge by what it is.** `ProjectedEdge::kind`:
  - `Edge`: between two faces that meet at an angle.
  - `Tangent`: between two faces whose ideal normals agree at the edge to
    within 1°, such as a fillet and its face. Only facets within 30° of
    each other are tested that way, since a search on each surface costs.
  - `Silhouette`: a seam inside one logical face where the face turns from
    the viewer to away from it.

  A facet seen exactly edge-on (an odd facet count has one in Front) is
  the outline itself: its seam with the neighbour facing the viewer is
  drawn, once. Any other seam inside a logical face is left out. Faces are one logical
  face when they share their ideal surface object, or when their names
  differ only by a facet or piece (`logicalFace`). Tangent edges are still
  drawn by the export, as before. The drawing sheet (148) will offer the
  choice.
- **Curves drawn as curves.**
  - A chord is drawn along the ideal curve it records when that curve runs
    between its ends. Two cases:
    - A primitive tags each chord with its own arc.
    - Extrude and Revolve tag every chord of a rim with the whole circle. Its
      span from one end to the other is found on it, the shorter way round.
      The review found that the first version took only an arc that started
      and ended at the chord's ends, so an extruded rim stayed a polygon.
  - A curve's pieces are emitted one by one, so a partly hidden curve stays
    curved. A run used to become one chord from its first point to its last.
  - A curve is cut into at least four pieces only where it strays visibly
    from its chord. A 2,048-gon's chords are already the curve.
- **Visibility that scales.**
  - A bounding-volume tree over the occluding triangles (median split,
    four per leaf), traversed with a fixed stack and no allocation.
  - Each edge is cut into pieces of 0.5% of the part's diagonal, from 1 to
    64, instead of always 24.
  - A 2,048-facet cylinder in isometric: **27 s → 0.69 s** in a Debug
    build.
- **Front looks from the front**: along +Y, with +X to the right, as the
  viewport's Front does. It looked from behind, mirrored.

### Tests
- A cylinder seen side-on: two silhouettes on its outline (within the
  facet sag), and its rims level. There are no other seams.
- A cylinder seen from above: every point of its rim is on the circle, to
  1e-9, and it strays from it by under 1e-3, where the chords strayed by
  0.014.
- An extruded circle's rim, seen from above, is on its circle too.
- A detail view keeps each edge's kind (it made every edge plain).
- A filleted box: its fillet's edges with the box are Tangent.
- A box's Front view spans x from 0 to 10, not from −10 to 0.
- A 2,048-facet cylinder projects in under 0.2 s optimized, or 2 s in
  Debug.
- All seven failed before the change. The existing projection, view,
  section and export tests pass unchanged.

## Phase 148: A drawing sheet

Built in two parts: the library, then the drawing tab, once the split (146)
gave the window a way in that is not `MainWindow` itself.

### Part 1 as built (library)
- **Views at a scale.** `DrawingView::scale` is sheet millimetres per model
  millimetre. `DrawingView::toSheet()` is the one mapping from view to
  sheet, `(p - boundsMin) * scale + placement`. It was written out four
  times, in the export and the dimension, balloon and tolerance renderers,
  none with a scale.
  - On a view drawn at a scale, a dimension states the length at 1:1, in
    the document's dimension style. The review found the first version used
    a default style, so a drawing set to inches showed millimetres. A
    per-dimension length factor, like DXF's DIMLFAC, belongs with 149.
  - A radial dimension's leader is its length on the sheet at any scale.
- **Views that choose what they show.** `showHidden` and `showTangentEdges`
  per view; the export honours both.
- **The sheet layout.** `DrawingGenerator::sheetLayout(solid, sheet,
  titleBlock)`:
  - the four standard views in third-angle projection: Top over Front,
    Right beside it, Isometric above that;
  - centred in the space above the title block;
  - at the largest ISO 5455 scale they fit (10:1 to 1:100; `scaleName()`
    writes "1:2");
  - tangent edges left out.
- **`DrawingExport::populate`** is public: the drawing, and the sheet's border
  and title block when given, into a 2D document on its layers. The drawing
  tab will show, print and export this.
- **`.hzdwg` version 2**:
  - It keeps the sheet (paper, orientation, margin), the title block's fields
    and each view's kind, direction, scale, placement and switches. Views are
    projected again from the part on load. No views saved gives the sheet
    layout.
  - The part is saved relative to the drawing's folder, so the two can move
    together. It was read from the working folder.
  - Every field is read by its type and checked: finite numbers, a positive
    scale, a direction that can be normalized. At most 256 views are read.
    Each is a projection of the whole part, and a small file asked for
    thousands. A part named by a number used
    to throw from the reader. A version of 1e300 was cast to int.
  - Version 1 files still read as the four standard views, spaced by their
    gap, at 1:1.
  - Loading says why it failed.

### Part 1 tests
- An A3 sheet of a 100 × 50 × 20 box:
  - drawn at 1:1, since 2:1 is too wide;
  - every view inside the border and above the title block, none
    overlapping;
  - Top aligned over Front, and Right beside it.
- A 2 m part is drawn at 1:10 or smaller, and a 5 mm part at 10:1.
- A drawing saved and read keeps its sheet, title block, views, placements
  and switches.
- A drawing and its part, moved together to another folder, still read.
- Hostile files are read with defaults or refused with a reason; none throw,
  and no edge comes out non-finite. 5,000 views are refused.
- A scaled view's dimension states inches in a drawing set to inches.
- A view at 1:2:
  - draws a 40 mm edge 20 mm long;
  - states it as 40;
  - leaves out the hidden and tangent edges it was told to.

### Part 2 as built (the drawing tab)
- **`DrawingWorkbench`**, the second workbench. Like the assembly one, it
  reaches the window only through `WorkbenchHost`, which gained `addTab`.
  It keeps each sheet's spec beside its tab's document.
- **Drawing ▸ New Drawing from Part** makes a sheet of the active part if
  it has been saved, or of a part file chosen. The sheet opens in a tab of
  its own, titled with the part, and is modified until it is saved.
- **The tab.**
  - It shows `populate`'s drawing on the sheet's own layers, locked.
  - It is seen from above with its paper in view whenever it is shown.
  - A redraw replaces only what is on those layers. What is drawn on the
    sheet by hand stays for the session, and the user's settings on its
    layers (shown or not, colours) survive the redraw.
  - Hand-drawn entities are not saved: the `.hzdwg` holds the spec only,
    and the status bar says so when a sheet with such entities is saved.
- **Drawn from the part as it is now.** The source is the part's tab when it
  is open and built, else its file.
- **Drawn again when the part changes.** The window's `refreshUsersOf`
  replaces its six calls to the assembly refresh, so drawings follow at the
  same moments assemblies do:
  - a part saved;
  - a part read again;
  - a part changed by another program;
  - a part closed with its edits discarded.

  Drawing ▸ Update from Part redraws on demand.
- **Files.**
  - A sheet saves as a `.hzdwg`. Save As adds the extension, since that is
    how it opens again.
  - File ▸ Open reads a `.hzdwg`. One already open shows its tab.
  - A sheet's own file changed by another program is read again as a
    sheet. It used to be read as a part, and failed.
  - A version 1 file keeps the layout it was drawn with: its views go into
    the spec, so saving it writes that layout as version 2. Saved without
    them, it would have opened again laid out anew.
- **Forms.**
  - Drawing ▸ Title Block: the fields, the paper and the orientation.
  - Drawing ▸ Scale: largest that fits, or a standard scale. The sheet is
    laid out again at that scale.
  - Each redraws the sheet and marks it modified.
- **Export.**
  - File ▸ Export ▸ DXF, PDF and SVG work on a sheet, as on any 2D tab.
  - The PDF and SVG form opens on the sheet's own paper at 1:1, because the
    sheet is already drawn to size. It used to open on A4, fitted.
- **Not in this part.**
  - Moving or scaling one view in the tab. The layout places them; per-view
    editing comes with section and detail views (149).
  - Recovery treats a sheet as any 2D document: one recovered after a crash
    comes back as a plain drawing of what it showed.

### Part 2 tests
- A sheet of the active part:
  - a new, modified tab titled with the part;
  - its layers locked, and seen from above with the A3 paper centred;
  - the title block names the part and states the scale.
- A sheet command outside a sheet says it needs one.
- Saved under a name with no extension, the file is a `.hzdwg` that names
  the part. Closed and opened again, it is the same sheet and not modified.
  Opened while open, its tab is shown.
- The Title Block form changes the fields and the paper, to A4 portrait,
  with the paper back in view. The Scale form draws it at 1:1, then 2:1.
- A sheet of a part chosen in the file dialog is drawn again when the part
  changes on disk, and when it is edited and saved in its tab.
- A sheet's own file changed on disk is read again, in the same tab and not
  modified.
- SVG export of a sheet with the form untouched: A3, border at 1:1.
- Without a window (a stand-in host):
  - a sheet opens locked;
  - it is drawn again from a changed part, keeping a hand-drawn line and a
    layer the user hid, and not made modified;
  - a sheet whose part is gone does not open, and says why;
  - a version 1 sheet saved and opened again draws the same.

## Phase 149: Section, detail and dimensions

Two parts. Part 1 adds views to a sheet: sections, details, and views moved
and removed by hand. Part 2 adds dimensions picked on the sheet, centre
lines, and a length factor for DXF.

### Part 1 as built: views added to a sheet
- **What a view is.** `model::DrawingView` gains:
  - a `role`: Projection, Section or Detail;
  - a `label`: "A" names section A-A, or detail A;
  - its `source`: the index of the view it was taken from, where its mark
    is drawn;
  - for a detail, the circle on the source, in the source's view space.

  A section's cut plane is its projection's: `origin`, looking along `dir`.
- **Built from the part.**
  - A section: `SectionGenerator::sectionView`, with hatch lines 3 mm apart
    on paper at any scale. They were 2 model mm apart, so at 1:10 the
    hatching was 0.2 mm apart on paper.
  - A detail: `DrawingGenerator::detailView` of its source as built, cropped
    and not enlarged. Its `scale` is its sheet scale, as any view's is.
- **Captions and marks**, on a new locked layer, `ViewLabels`:
  - "SECTION A-A" under a section. On its source, the cut as a chain line,
    with arrows at both ends pointing the way the section looks, and the
    letter by each.
  - "DETAIL B (2:1)" under a detail. On its source, a circle with the
    letter.
- **Placed where there is room.** `DrawingGenerator::freePlacement` finds
  the first spot for a view of a given size that is:
  - inside the border;
  - clear of the title block;
  - at least the gap from every view and its caption.

  Where there is none, the view goes beside the sheet, and the user is told.
- **`.hzdwg` version 3**:
  - Each view's role, label, source and detail circle are saved.
  - A detail or section whose source is not a projection view before it is
    refused with a reason.
  - Versions 1 and 2 read as before.
- **Commands** (Drawing menu):
  - **Add Section View**: a form. Choose the view, a vertical or horizontal
    cut, where it cuts (from the view's centre), which way it looks, and
    the label. A cut that misses the part is refused.
  - **Add Detail View**: click the centre on a view, then the radius, as a
    circle is drawn. A form then takes the scale (twice the view's, by
    default) and the label.
  - **Move View**: click a view, then where it goes.
  - **Remove View**: a form. The views taken from it go too, and the user
    is told.
  - An automatic sheet becomes explicit views when the first view is added.
- **The Scale form keeps added views.**
  - The standard views are laid out again at the chosen scale.
  - A section takes that scale; a detail keeps its ratio to its source.
  - Added views are placed again where there is room.
- **Host.** `WorkbenchHost` gains `runTool` and `endTool`.
  - `PickTool` takes clicks for a command, each offered to it, and refused
    with a reason when wrong ("Not on a view").
  - It hands the points over on the release of the last click. The command
    then ends the tool out of the tool's own event, and no release is left
    over to reach the Select tool.
- **Scales stay standard.** A detail re-scaled with the sheet goes to the
  standard scale at or above its old ratio to its view. `scaleName` writes
  a scale that is not whole compactly ("2.5:1", not "2.500000:1").
- **Not in this part.** Moving a projected view does not move the views
  aligned with it.

### Part 1 tests
- **Library.**
  - A view added to a sheet is placed inside the border, clear of the title
    block, and the gap from every view and caption. The next one goes
    elsewhere. One too large goes beside the sheet.
  - `.hzdwg` 3 keeps a section and a detail. Built again, the section is
    cut and hatched, and the detail keeps only what is inside its circle,
    not enlarged.
  - A detail of a later view, a detail with no radius, and a section of a
    section are refused with reasons. A long label with a line break is
    cut short.
  - The export captions a section "SECTION A-A" and a detail with its
    scale. It draws the cut on its source as one vertical chain line past
    the view, with a letter at each end, and the detail's circle on its
    source.
- **Window.**
  - A section through Front is captioned, hatched, saved and opened again.
    A cut 500 mm off the part is refused.
  - A detail:
    - a click off every view is refused;
    - clicked at Front's corner, it is circled there;
    - it is drawn at twice the view's scale by default;
    - Select is the tool again after it.
  - A view moved by two clicks moves by as much, within a pixel's snap.
    The other views stay.
  - Removing Front takes its detail with it, and says so.
  - At 1:1 the Scale form keeps the section, at 1:1, and the detail, at a
    standard scale. Each is placed with room.
- **Without a window.** The tool refuses a click off every view. It does
  not move the view while the button is down, and the tool is ended after
  its event, not inside it.
